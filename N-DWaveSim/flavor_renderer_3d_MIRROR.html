What this validates: the Stage-1 polar renderer lifted to convex solids. One expression r(u) = a / maxᵢ(u·nᵢ) drives the cube; the sphere is r = R; the p-norm slider sweeps octahedron (p = 1) → sphere (p = 2) → cube (p = ∞) with the same mesh topology throughout. In 3D the signature is a vector (₁R/₀R, ₂R/₀R), not a scalar — cube and octahedron share the face entry and differ only in the edge entry. The mesh is a cube-sphere (six N×N grids scaled radially), chosen so the axis, edge-midpoint and body-diagonal directions are exact vertices: min/max radii are then measured, not interpolated, and the (+,+,+) octant patch is an exact sub-mesh. Volume and area come from the drawn triangles (divergence theorem), never from the closed forms; curved flavors show their chordal error and its observed convergence order. (The ledger integrates the Float64 vertex array; the GPU draws its Float32 copy — the two differ by float32 rounding, ≤ 6·10−8 relative.) The [111] ghost is the octant patch projected along the body diagonal: for the cube it must be the three-rhombus hexagon of the LatMosaic atom, circumradius (edge/2)·√(2/3). The snap button puts the camera on a body diagonal — the parent Stage-2 demo (eight vertices → six at 60°), measured through the actual camera projection. Rotor code is the verified Cl(3,0) implementation (e₃₁ storage sign convention included) and its agreement with the three.js quaternion is itself a ledger row.
