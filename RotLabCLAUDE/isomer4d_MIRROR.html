What this is. A chiral ("handed") 3D object cannot be rotated into its mirror image in 3D — but in 4D the mirror is an ordinary rotation, in the plane spanned by one 3D axis and the 4th axis w (Möbius, 1827). Embed the object at w=0, rotate by θ in the (x, w) plane, and project back by dropping w: every point's x becomes x·cos θ. The 3D shadow squashes along the flip axis, passes through a completely flat state at 90° (the 3D analogue of the card seen edge-on), and re-inflates as the enantiomer — the same cos θ law as the card_flip app, one dimension up. In ZRL vocabulary: dimension n's unreachable orientation class (isomer) is dimension n+1's rotation; the V₄ element you cannot reach becomes a path. The open tribar preset is on-theme: flipping it through 4D reverses its winding — the two Penrose-triangle chiralities are 4D-rotations of each other. Note what the ghosts show while you orbit: at θ=90° the object coincides with the mirror of itself, which is exactly why the crossing is possible.
