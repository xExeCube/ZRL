What this is. The impossible hexagons close by lying about one joint; the helix tells the truth: a hexagonal walk with sides (L+k, L, L+k, L, L+k, L) climbs exactly k·(1,1,1) per lap, so every lap projects to the same closed hexagon (checked live) and the 3D object is an honest infinite staircase — the Penrose stairs' legitimate sibling. Down the axis you see ONE hexagon no matter how many laps exist (the top lap hides the rest; the lap shear slider explodes the stack to reveal them). The y-side / x-side views answer what the ring looks like from the side: a climbing zigzag coil. Triangle connectors: any path joining a point on one lap to the point k·(1,1,1) above it has edge-sum k(1,1,1), which projects to a CLOSED loop — the minimal x,y,z staircase connector projects to exactly a unit triangle. Connections between laps are therefore triangle-flavored by necessity: in the axis view every connector appears as a small closed triangle decorating the ring (the tribar corner motif is the only currency inter-lap rungs can be paid in). Multi-start: a k-climb helix admits exactly k parallel interleaved threads (shifts by j·(1,1,1), j < k) — k=2 is a double helix, k=3 a triple; the slider clamps to k and the ledger counts collisions (0 when legal).
