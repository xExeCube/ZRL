What this is. Beams of unit cubes walk through ℤ³ and are flattened along the cube diagonal: cell (x,y,z) projects to the hexagon at lattice vertex (x−y, y−z), its three visible faces (+x, +y, +z) landing as the three 60°/120° rhombus orientations, two atoms each — the ZRL canon's cube→hexagon fact made into a renderer. Hidden surfaces are resolved per atom: every face's depth is a linear function of position whose pairwise order cannot change inside an atom (face-plane intersection lines project onto atom edges — the ledger re-verifies this on live samples), so the deepest face at the atom centroid is exactly the visible one. The illusion: where two beams contest the same atoms (a crossing — 3D positions differing by a multiple of (1,1,1)), the winner can be flipped per beam pair, coherently: 0% is honest geometry, 100% inverts every crossing, anything between is a seeded coin-flip per pair — Reutersvärd weaving. Solid 3D joints (beams meeting corner-to-corner) share atoms too, but are never flippable: only true same-column crossings carry a coin, so elbows always render as solid unions. The tribar preset is the Penrose triangle: three arms +x, +y, +z whose 3D closure error (L−1)·(1,1,1) projects to zero, so the honest renderer already draws an impossible figure (closure measured live in the ledger). The knot walk grows a random axis-turning beam chain that is bribed (crossing bias) into revisiting its own projected columns at different depths. Click a beam to paint it — window beams render flat chroma for GIMP masks; mask png = white where window beams own the pixel. Same seeds → same figure. Hex tiling vs tight tiling: both lay an n×n honeycomb of independent impossible hex rings (chirality alternating by parity; genuinely impossible rings cannot edge-share, so they abut). The loose layout leaves a connected 3-cell channel between all rings (open moat — image shows through everywhere); the TIGHT layout (pitches 2L+1 / 2L+3, verified on the torus for L=3..8) closes the moat completely except two enclosed solid-lozenge windows per 2-ring period — (L−1)×(L+1) and L×(L+2) diamonds, each bordered by exactly 4 rings: crisp rhombus cutouts for the overlay, the rings + rhombi plane tiling. Abut tiling: disjoint rings joined leg-to-leg along chains — consecutive rings abut along their parallel slanted legs, chirality alternating — and chains stacked so a ring's horizontal leg and the next ring's meet corner-to-corner, collinear. Chain step (2L+2, −(L+1)), stack step (0, 2L+2). Its windows are TRUE 60° rhombi of side L and L+1 alternating (2L² and 2(L+1)² atoms, one per ring, each bordered by 4 rings); the wobble's long leg makes the bigger rhombus. The ledger counts atoms shared between rings live (must be 0 in these three layouts). Chain tiling (the user's 6-bar + 4-bar tiling) is different in kind: a FUSED structure. Every 6-bar is an identical copy; down a column each 6-bar's top bar IS the bar above's bottom bar (the double-hex shared-bar mechanism, chained), and side by side the columns share the side-vertex cube where two straight bar-lines cross in an X — shared bars and vertex cubes exist once, so the whole tiling is one connected set of cubes (column step (1,−L,L+1), column pitch (L,−L,−L) in ℤ³). The windows are identical lozenges of interior sides L−1 and L−2, and each one, read as a 4-bar, closes in 3D except for exactly ONE inherited false joint (−1,0,−1): the rhombus is a genuine impossible 4-bar (its bars are L+1, L, L, L — a wobbled rhombus, which is why this 4-cycle can be impossible while an equal-sided one cannot). The ledger re-walks every 6-bar against the scene: all sides present, closure k=−1 measured for every complete ring, and the shared bars / vertex cubes counted from cell ownership. Packed honeycomb: the densest packing of disjoint 6-bars (abut chain with stack (L, L+2)). Its only gaps are single atoms — exactly one unit triangle per ring, where three rings meet, at every L. That atom is the wobble's irreducible residue: a regular hexagon would tile with no gap at all, but a regular 6-bar cannot be impossible (its false joints would have to cancel), so one atom per ring is as close as an impossible honeycomb gets to gapless. Sierpiński 3-bar: the fractal bar-sharing lattice — three sub-3-bars at the corners of a 3-bar of twice the arm (minus one), sharing their corner cubes pairwise, recursively (arm slider = smallest 3-bar, tiling-size slider = depth+1; total arm 2d(m−1)+1). One connected cube set with no lifts; every leaf closes by column overlap, and every assembled triangle cycle is impossible too: the outline of a depth-d gasket has 2d column-overlap joints (one per leaf on its hypotenuse) and a hole opened at level l has 2l−1 (one in the smallest holes) — the closure hierarchy's “triangle cycles are always impossible” made fractal. The ledger re-walks every leaf against the scene and counts the shared corner cubes (3 per internal node). 3-bar strip / 3-bar tiling: the triangular tiling of alternating up and down 3-bars in which EVERY bar is shared by one up and one down triangle (the double-hex mechanism applied to 3-bars; the strip keeps one row). The up-triangle lattice T1 = (−(L−1), −L, 0), T2 = (−(L−1), −(2L−2), −L) follows from the cell equations; one connected cube set with zero unintended coincidences; up triangles close by +(L−1)(1,1,1), down ones by −(L−1)(1,1,1) — all column overlaps, so every triangle is impossible and every closing pair is a true crossing (the flip slider works per triangle). Each triangle encloses (L−4)² atoms for arm ≥ 4: solid at arms 3 and 4, one-atom windows at 5, 9-atom windows at 7. Colour by line (Style): every beam is coloured by the projected lattice line it lies on, so collinear continuations across shared bars and vertex cubes share a colour — the Sierpiński gasket's three outer edges become three colours, and in the chain tiling each column of legs and the 4-bar windows they bound read as one line. Kagome (loose): a strict kagome of 6-bars — every ring sharing corner cubes with six neighbours around triangular holes — does not exist: in projection an all-identical kagome fails locally (each ring presents sides (L−1, L, L) to one triangle class and (L, L+1, L+1) to the other, so no hole is equal-sided), mixing the 12 rotation / mirror / chirality ring variants does not rescue it (single holes can close, but no assignment of variants to a radius-3 patch of 37 rings closes every hole — exhaustive search), and, independently, every projectively closed triangular hole carries a nonzero 3D residue. The realizable relative shares corner cubes in two of the three directions and is loose by one column in the third; its holes are triangles of (L−3)² and (L−2)² atoms, one of each per ring for arm ≥ 4 (at arm 3 the smaller hole closes to a solid corner), each an impossible 3-bar whose whole residue sits at the loose vertex. Double 6-bar honeycomb: alternating-chirality double 6-bars on the lattice T1=(L+4, 2−L, 3), T2=(L−1, L+1, −(2L+2)) cover the plane with no gap atom at all — the only uncovered atoms are the ring interiors (checked per fundamental domain, L=4, 6, 8). The sharing is sparse: each double unit shares its own L−1 bar (the double-hex mechanism) and one L bar with its column neighbour (A's +y bar is that neighbour's B −y bar, each ring's corner cube sitting one step past the other's bar end); every other bar abuts a neighbouring bar outer-edge to outer-edge, the two bars' cubes two lattice steps apart in projection so no atom is left between them. A rings k=−1, B rings k=+1, no crossings. View axis (isomer views): every figure here is a real set of unit cubes in ℤ³; the picture is its projection along the body diagonal (1,1,1). The other three body diagonals are lattice-equivalent view directions: the same cubes under a lattice rotation, rendered the same way. Along (1,1,−1) and (−1,1,1) a 6-bar opens into a bow-tie whose two ends no longer meet (two lattice steps apart); along (1,−1,1) it is a self-crossing bow-tie whose ends still meet at a false joint (D=(−1,0,−1) = −y − (1,−1,1)) — the same sculpture is impossible from two of the four lattice directions. The double 6-bar becomes the "two open rings joined by a bar" of the card_spin3d tumble, and the honeycomb rows of such hourglasses (a different lattice from the catalogue's hourglass chain, whose period (L,1,−L) shares two bars). The ledger keeps auditing closures and fused units in the (1,1,1) frame, where the identities live; the rendered-scene checks follow the view. Cube cells only.
